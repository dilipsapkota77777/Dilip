# NDIS Referral Partner Checklist
## 7 Questions Before You Formalise Any Referral Relationship
*From Helpingme | For NDIS Providers Building Sustainable Partner Networks*

---

## Why Most NDIS Referral Partnerships Fail

Most NDIS providers have referral partners on paper.
Few have referral partners who actually send consistent participants.

The difference isn't the relationship — it's the process.
A coffee meeting creates goodwill. A shared intake process creates flow.

This checklist gives you 7 questions to ask before committing to any referral relationship — so you stop wasting time on partnerships that sound good but deliver nothing.

---

## The 7 Questions

### Question 1: Do they serve a complementary participant type — or the same one?

A referral partnership only works if one provider's overflow becomes the other's opportunity.

- ✅ **Green:** They serve psychosocial participants, you serve physical disability — participants often need both
- ✅ **Green:** They offer Allied Health, you offer Core Supports — complementary and non-competing
- ⚠️ **Yellow:** Some overlap — possible conflict if you're competing for the same participants
- ❌ **Red:** Same services, same participant types — this is a competitor, not a partner

**Ask them directly:** "Who is a participant you regularly can't serve or have to refer out? And who sends you the most referrals currently?"

---

### Question 2: Do they have an established participant flow — or are they also looking for participants?

A provider with no incoming participants can't refer to you.

Look for:
- Been operating 2+ years (established flow)
- 10+ employees (team to generate and manage referrals)
- Support Coordinators, Allied Health, or Plan Managers in their network — these roles actively place participants

**Red flag:** A startup provider pitching you as their primary referral source. That's not a partnership — that's outsourcing their business development to you.

---

### Question 3: Is there a clear intake process — or just a phone number?

The difference between a referral relationship that works and one that doesn't is usually the intake.

**Minimum viable intake process:**
- A shared intake form (or a clear referral template)
- One named contact on each side (not "call the office")
- Agreed response time (e.g., "we'll confirm capacity within 24 hours")
- Agreement on what information travels with the participant

If a potential partner can't answer "how would I actually refer a participant to you?" in a clear process — they're not ready.

---

### Question 4: What's their geographic coverage?

A Melbourne provider can't take your Sydney participant.

Confirm:
- Their primary service region
- Whether they travel / use remote support workers
- Any suburbs or LGAs they won't cover

**Opportunity:** Providers who serve adjacent regions (e.g., they cover inner-west Sydney, you cover Western Sydney) are ideal partners — shared geography, no competition.

---

### Question 5: What are their registration groups?

NDIS registration groups determine what a provider can legally deliver and be paid for.

Before committing to a referral relationship:
- Ask which registration groups they hold
- Confirm they can actually deliver what you'd be sending them
- Check NDIS provider register if you're unsure: ndiscommission.gov.au/providers/registered-providers

**Why this matters:** Sending a participant to a provider who isn't registered for the right support category creates a compliance risk for the participant and for you.

---

### Question 6: How do they handle capacity constraints?

Every provider hits capacity. The question is what happens when they do.

**Ask them:** "When you can't take a new participant, what do you do? Do you have a waitlist? Do you refer to someone else? Who?"

What you want to hear:
- They have a clear process (waitlist, backup provider, honest communication timeline)
- They'll tell you if they can't take someone rather than letting the participant fall through

**Red flag:** "We always have capacity." No established provider always has capacity — this means they either don't understand their own operation or they'll say yes and then not deliver.

---

### Question 7: Are they willing to track it?

The partnerships that produce consistent participant flow are the ones both parties review.

**Minimum tracking:**
- Monthly or quarterly count: how many referrals sent each way
- Conversion rate: how many referrals actually converted to participants
- Any quality feedback: did the participant feel the transition was smooth?

It doesn't need to be a spreadsheet. Even a monthly 10-minute check-in with a simple count works.

If a potential partner isn't willing to track and review — the relationship will naturally drift to whoever needs something in the moment, not a sustainable two-way flow.

---

## Quick Scoring

Go through the 7 questions with any potential partner. Score each one:
- ✅ Green answer = 2 points
- ⚠️ Yellow answer = 1 point
- ❌ Red answer = 0 points

**Total score:**
- **12–14 points:** Formalise immediately — this is a high-value partner
- **8–11 points:** Worth trialling with low commitment first — one referral each way, review after 60 days
- **4–7 points:** Proceed cautiously — address the gaps before formalising
- **0–3 points:** Not ready — re-evaluate in 6 months

---

## What a Formalised Referral Partnership Looks Like

You don't need a lengthy contract. You need 4 things written down:

1. **Services we'll refer to each other** — list them clearly
2. **Participant types we'll refer to each other** — specific, not vague
3. **Our intake process** — the exact steps when a referral happens
4. **How we'll review it** — monthly or quarterly, who initiates

One page. Signed by a decision-maker on each side. That's it.
